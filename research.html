Research program / Information over time

The web changes. Its evidence changes with it.

Studying the web as a changing record.

My research examines how online information is captured, replayed, described, and interpreted—and how those processes shape what can later be known.

Section 02 / Core questions

What survives—and what does it mean?

The work moves between technical behavior and human interpretation, asking how an archive becomes evidence rather than merely storage.

01

Representation

Whose web did the archive capture?

Personalized pages, advertisements, scripts, and platform behavior complicate the idea of a single canonical web experience.

  • Personalization
  • Replay fidelity
  • Dynamic content

02

Meaning

How does language evolve across time?

Terms and concepts drift between disciplines. Detecting change is only the first step; documenting provenance makes it scholarly evidence.

  • Semantic shift
  • Knowledge organization
  • Provenance

03

Infrastructure

What makes a record durable and reusable?

Persistent identifiers, open tools, metadata, and accountable workflows help research records remain intelligible beyond a single project.

  • Digital libraries
  • Persistent systems
  • Open scholarship

Section 03 / Methods

Technical inquiry with a documentary conscience.

Projects combine system building, empirical analysis, metadata design, and interpretive evaluation. Each method leaves a record of its own decisions.
